About the model

What this is

A planning and teaching tool for the shading study: choose obstruction sizes and positions for test days and explain the method. In the thesis, shading loss is estimated from paired electrical records (test frame vs. reference frame × k̂), not predicted from geometry. Numbers here are illustrative.

Geometry

Frame outlines, tilts, heights and spacing come from the 14 Sep 2026 LiDAR plane fits (three sessions). The scan is not georeferenced, so each session is rotated so Frame A faces due south (protocol orientation); positions relative to Frame A are exactly as scanned. The surroundings (ground, pad, fence, power pole, building, vegetation) are the merged raw scans of all three sessions; the scans hold geometry only, so colours are inferred from local shape using a street-level photo of the site. Racks are modelled to dimensions measured in the scans. Only the obstruction you add (and optionally the scanned power pole) casts shadows in the calculation.

Sun and clear sky

NOAA solar position (agrees with NREL SPA to about 0.01°), Ineichen clear sky with monthly Linke turbidity, Hay–Davies sky diffuse and 0.2 ground albedo. Pacific time with daylight saving.

Production

Shading loss (approximation)

Series 6 Plus modules are long, narrow CdTe cells in series with no bypass diodes, so the string current follows the most-shaded stripe. Each stripe receives beam + circumsolar light times the fraction that passes the obstruction, plus sky diffuse (minus the part of the sky the obstruction hides) and ground reflection. Output = weakest stripe ÷ unshaded. Leaves let some light through (canopy density). This is a first-order approximation, not a cell-level circuit model.

Daily view

Tested window: sun above the set elevation. QW = unshaded − actual energy in the window (shading + any operating constraint), Q1h its hourly split, and daily % = QW ÷ E0,W, rated against your limits.
